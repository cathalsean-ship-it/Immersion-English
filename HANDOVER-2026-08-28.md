## The structural change, and why

Four of today's bugs were the same bug: a fact typed into copy instead of read from data, then drifting when the data changed. `llms.txt` claimed ages 12–18 while `dublin.ts` said 12–17; it said "1, 2, 3 or 4 weeks" after the programme became a fixed two; it advertised a free pre-departure course that commit `40b558a` removed from the included list months earlier.

So:

- **`public/llms.txt` is gone.** It is now `src/pages/llms.txt.ts`, an endpoint that builds the same file at the same URL from `dublin.ts` and `courses.ts`. Programme facts, included/not-included, excursions and supervision all render from data.
- **`src/data/courses.ts` is new.** Course prices used to exist in both `lessons.astro` and `llms.txt`. Both now render from this file. `lessons.astro`'s built HTML is byte-identical to what production served beforehand, so it is a pure refactor.
- **`courses.ts` fails the build** if a bundle price does not match its advertised saving. Verified by reintroducing the old €215 bundle, which stops the build with: *"the 5-lesson bundle is €215, but 5% off 5 × €45 is €213."*

**If you are about to type a price, an age, a duration or a course fee into a template: put it in `dublin.ts` or `courses.ts` and interpolate it.** That is the whole point.

## Still open, in the order I would do them

### 1. The €2,690 has never been costed — business decision, not code

It is live on the site. It was set by comparing published competitor pricing for two-week Dublin junior programmes, not by checking what MLA will charge. The comment in `dublin.ts` says so plainly: *"NOT yet checked against MLA's 2027 net rate — do that before this goes anywhere near a parent."*

If MLA's net rate lands above roughly €2,000 a head, the margin is thin once you count the twelve-student threshold and your own travel. This is a phone call, not a commit. **Nothing else on this list can cost real money.**

### 2. Every missing URL redirects to itself forever

Not a 404 — an infinite loop. A page that has never existed returns 301 to itself; curl gives up after 51 hops. Cause is the only rule in `netlify.toml`:

```toml
[[redirects]]
  from = "/*/"
  to = "/:splat"
  status = 301
```

It means to strip trailing slashes, but Netlify's own pretty-URL handling adds them — `/contact` → 301 → `/contact/` → 200. Real pages survive the fight; missing ones loop. There is also **no 404 page at all** (`src/pages/404.astro` does not exist).

Likely fix: delete the rule, add a 404 page. **Verify trailing-slash behaviour on real pages before and after** — that rule touches every URL on the site, so this is the one change here that could affect indexing.

### 3. Images — the site's weakest area

Graded D this session, and it is the honest weak point.

- The accommodation gallery is **2.6MB of raw JPEGs** (down from 5.4MB after the trim). They sit in `public/`, which Astro's image optimiser never touches. Moving them to `src/assets/` and rendering through `<Image>` should get the page to a few hundred KB. This is the change that actually fixes the grade.
- `og-default.webp` is 1.7MB and `teens-dublin.webp` is 1.6MB. Both are served.
- **96MB of unreferenced original JPEGs** sit in `public/` — `students-walking.jpg` at 9.6MB, `glendalough-lake.jpg` at 9.5MB. No visitor downloads them, but they are publicly reachable and deploy every time. Treat deletion as a careful batch job: some are the sources for images that *are* live, and their URLs may be indexed.

## Things that will confuse you

**Netlify rewrites `<form>` tags at deploy.** A naive diff of `dist/` against the live site shows the homepage, contact and corporate-training pages as changed even when nothing changed — Netlify strips `data-netlify` and `netlify-honeypot`, reorders attributes and switches to single quotes. Normalise `<form[^>]*>` before diffing or you will chase a ghost. Pages without forms diff cleanly.

**Alt text can be wrong while the code looks perfect.** Three gallery images described the wrong photograph — `nci-07` is a bedroom labelled "Shared living room", `nci-11` a kitchen labelled "Breakout room". Invisible from the source. I only caught it by building a contact sheet and looking. If you touch image metadata, open the images.

**The comments in `dublin.ts` are load-bearing.** They record *why* things were removed and what must not come back — the age range, the contracting entity, the Sofia travel claim. They have already stopped bad copy from being reinstated more than once. Keep writing them in that style, and read them before changing the data they guard.
